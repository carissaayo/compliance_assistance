SECTION 1: 2026 TAX REFORM RELIEFS AND EXEMPTIONS SUMMARY
================================================================================

From 1 January 2026, the new tax laws provide many reliefs and exemptions for
low-income earners, average taxpayers, and small businesses.

PERSONAL INCOME TAX / PAYE
---------------------------
- Individuals earning the national minimum wage or less: EXEMPT
- Annual gross income up to NGN 1,200,000 (approx. NGN 800,000 taxable income): EXEMPT
- Reduced PAYE tax for those earning annual gross income up to NGN 20 million
- Gifts: EXEMPT

ALLOWABLE DEDUCTIONS AND RELIEFS FOR INDIVIDUALS
-------------------------------------------------
- Pension contribution to PFA (Pension Fund Administrator)
- National Health Insurance Scheme (NHIS)
- National Housing Fund (NHF) contributions
- Interest on loans for owner-occupied residential housing
- Life insurance or annuity premiums
- Rent relief: 20% of annual rent (up to NGN 500,000)

PENSIONS AND GRATUITIES — EXEMPT
---------------------------------
- Pension funds and assets under the Pension Reform Act (PRA) are tax-exempt
- Pension, gratuity or any retirement benefits granted in line with the PRA
- Compensation for loss of employment up to NGN 50 million

CAPITAL GAINS TAX (CGT) — EXEMPT
----------------------------------
- Sale of an owner-occupied house
- Personal effects or chattels worth up to NGN 5 million
- Sale of up to two private vehicles per year
- Gains on shares below NGN 150 million per year or gains up to NGN 10 million
- Gains on shares above exemption threshold if proceed is reinvested
- Pension funds, charities, and religious institutions (non-commercial)

COMPANIES INCOME TAX (CIT) — EXEMPT/RELIEF
--------------------------------------------
- Small companies (turnover not more than NGN 100 million AND total fixed assets
  not more than NGN 250 million): 0% tax rate
- Eligible (labelled) startups: EXEMPT
- Compensation relief: 50% additional deduction for salary increases, wage
  awards, or transport subsidies for low-income workers
- Employment relief: 50% deduction for salaries of new employees hired and
  retained for at least three years
- Tax holiday for first 5 years for agricultural businesses (crop production,
  livestock, dairy, etc.)
- Gains from investment in a labelled startup by venture capitalist, private
  equity fund, accelerators or incubators

DEVELOPMENT LEVY — EXEMPT
--------------------------
- Small companies are exempt from 4% development levy

WITHHOLDING TAX — EXEMPT
-------------------------
- Small companies, manufacturers and agric businesses are exempt from
  withholding tax deduction on their income
- Small companies are exempt from deduction on their payments to suppliers

VALUE ADDED TAX (VAT) — 0% OR EXEMPT
--------------------------------------
- Basic food items: 0% VAT
- Rent: EXEMPT
- Education services and materials: 0% VAT
- Health and medical services: EXEMPT
- Pharmaceutical products: 0% VAT
- Small companies (NGN 100m or less turnover): exempt from charging VAT
- Diesel, petrol, and solar power equipment: VAT suspended or exempt
- Refund of VAT on assets and overheads to produce VATable or 0% VAT goods
- Agricultural inputs (fertilizers, seeds, seedlings, feeds, live animals)
- Purchase, lease or hire of equipment for agric purposes
- Disability aids (hearing aids, wheelchairs, braille materials)
- Transport: shared passenger road transport (non-charter)
- Electric vehicles and parts: EXEMPT
- Humanitarian supplies: EXEMPT
- Baby products
- Sanitary towels, pads or tampons
- Land and building

STAMP DUTIES — EXEMPT
----------------------
- Electronic money transfers below NGN 10,000
- Salary payments
- Intra-bank transfers
- Transfers of government securities or shares
- All documents for transfer of stocks and shares

SECTION 3: INTRODUCTION — NIGERIA PAYE SYSTEM
================================================================================

Nigeria's personal income tax is governed by the Personal Income Tax Act
(PITA), with PAYE deducted at source by employers for all employees working in
Nigeria. The Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) oversees federal employees,
while State Internal Revenue Services administer PAYE for employees in their
respective states.

PITA rates are uniform nationally — states do not set different income tax
rates. However, PAYE for private sector employees is remitted to the State IRS
of the state where the employee works (not where the employer is registered).

The defining feature of Nigeria's PAYE system is the Consolidated Relief
Allowance (CRA): before any brackets are applied, employees deduct the higher
of 20% of gross income or NGN 200,000, plus an additional fixed NGN 200,000.
Pension contributions (8% under the CPS) and National Housing Fund (NHF)
contributions (2.5% of basic salary) further reduce taxable income.

Brackets apply to taxable income after CRA, pension, NHF, and other approved
deductions have been subtracted from gross income.

--------------------------------|---------------|----------------
NGN 0 – 300,000                 | 7%            | NGN 21,000
NGN 300,001 – 600,000           | 11%           | NGN 33,000
NGN 600,001 – 1,100,000         | 15%           | NGN 75,000
NGN 1,100,001 – 1,600,000       | 19%           | NGN 95,000
NGN 1,600,001 – 3,200,000       | 21%           | NGN 336,000
Above NGN 3,200,000             | 24%           | Uncapped

Minimum Tax Rule:
  If total PAYE is less than 1% of gross income, PAYE is set at 1% of gross.
  This typically only affects employees with very high deductions relative to
  income.

State vs. Federal:
  PITA rates are uniform across all 36 states and the FCT. PAYE for private
  sector employees is remitted to the State IRS of the state where the employee
  works — not where the employer is registered.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATED RELIEF ALLOWANCE (CRA) EXPLAINED
================================================================================

The CRA is the cornerstone of Nigeria's PITA and the primary reason effective
tax rates are well below headline brackets for most formal sector workers.

CRA Formula:
  The higher of:
    (a) 1% of gross income, OR
    (b) NGN 200,000
  PLUS 20% of gross income as an additional allowance.

In practice, for almost all employees:
  CRA = NGN 200,000 + 20% of gross income

Examples:
  At NGN 2,400,000/year gross (NGN 200,000/month):
    CRA = NGN 200,000 + (20% x NGN 2,400,000) = NGN 680,000

  At NGN 6,000,000/year gross (NGN 500,000/month):
    CRA = NGN 200,000 + (20% x NGN 6,000,000) = NGN 1,400,000

  At NGN 12,000,000/year gross (NGN 1,000,000/month):
    CRA = NGN 200,000 + (20% x NGN 12,000,000) = NGN 2,600,000

Pension contributions are also fully deductible, making them tax-efficient.

SECTION 7: PENSION (CPS) AND NHF PAYROLL DEDUCTIONS
================================================================================

CONTRIBUTORY PENSION SCHEME (CPS) — Employee: 8%
-------------------------------------------------
Under the Pension Reform Act 2014, employees contribute 8% of monthly total
emoluments (basic salary + housing allowance + transport allowance) to a
Retirement Savings Account (RSA) with a Pension Fund Administrator (PFA) of
their choice.

Employer contributes a minimum of 10% (some contribute 12% or higher).
Contributions are invested by the PFA in a diversified portfolio.
The employer remits combined contributions monthly to the selected PFA.
Pension contributions are FULLY DEDUCTIBLE from PAYE taxable income.

NATIONAL HOUSING FUND (NHF) — Employee: 2.5% of basic salary
-------------------------------------------------------------
Employees in the public sector and private companies with 10 or more employees
contribute 2.5% of basic monthly salary to the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria
(FMBN).

NHF contributions are TAX-DEDUCTIBLE.
NHF contributions accumulate and can be accessed through NHF-backed mortgage
loans for residential property purchase.
NHF is distinct from pension — it is a housing-specific fund.

NSITF — Employer Only: 1% of total emoluments
----------------------------------------------
The National Social Insurance Trust Fund (NSITF) requires employers to
contribute 1% of total emoluments for workplace injury insurance.
This is an EMPLOYER-ONLY cost and does not reduce employee take-home pay.

SECTION 8: PAYE FILING — EMPLOYER AND EMPLOYEE OBLIGATIONS
================================================================================

Monthly PAYE Remittance:
  PAYE deducted must be remitted to the relevant State IRS by the 10th of the
  following month.

Annual Employer Return:
  File annual PAYE return (Form H1) with State IRS by 31 January.
  Shows total PAYE deducted from each employee during the preceding year.

Employee Annual Return:
  Employees with non-PAYE income (business, rental, investment) must file an
  annual return with their State IRS by 31 March.
  PAYE withheld is credited against the annual liability.

TIN Registration:
  All employees must have a Tax Identification Number (TIN).
  Registration: tin.firs.gov.ng or State IRS offices.
  Required for banking, property transactions, vehicle registration, and most
  formal commercial activities.

Tax Clearance Certificate (TCC):
  Required for government contracts, importing, and many professional licences.
  Apply through State IRS or FIRS after filing all returns and settling
  outstanding tax. Issued for three years.

SECTION 9: FAQ — NIGERIA PAYE
================================================================================

Q: What is Nigeria PAYE?
A: PAYE (Pay As You Earn) is Nigeria's system of withholding personal income
tax from employees' salaries at source. Employers calculate monthly PAYE using
PITA brackets (applied after deducting CRA, pension, and NHF), deduct it from
gross salary, and remit to the relevant State IRS (or FIRS for federal
employees) by the 10th of the following month. The legal framework is PITA.

Q: What is the Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) in Nigeria?
A: The CRA is a mandatory deduction from gross employment income before PAYE
brackets are applied. It equals NGN 200,000 plus 20% of gross annual income.
For example, at NGN 6,000,000 gross: CRA = NGN 200,000 + NGN 1,200,000 =
NGN 1,400,000. The CRA significantly reduces taxable income.

Q: How do I calculate my Nigeria PAYE?
A: (1) Determine annual gross income. (2) Compute CRA: NGN 200,000 + 20% of
gross. (3) Subtract pension (8% of total emoluments) and NHF (2.5% of basic
salary). (4) Subtract CRA to get taxable income. (5) Apply PITA brackets:
7% on first NGN 300,000; 11% on NGN 300,001–600,000; 15% on NGN 600,001–1.1M;
19% on NGN 1.1M–1.6M; 21% on NGN 1.6M–3.2M; 24% above NGN 3.2M.

Q: What are pension contributions in Nigeria?
A: Under the CPS, employees contribute 8% of monthly total emoluments to an
RSA with a PENCOM-licensed PFA. Employers contribute a minimum of 10%.
Contributions are fully deductible from PAYE taxable income.

Q: Does the state you work in affect your Nigeria PAYE rate?
A: The PITA tax brackets and CRA are uniform across all 36 states and the FCT.
However, PAYE for private sector employees is administered by the State IRS of
the state where the employee works (not where the employer is registered).

Q: What is Nigeria's top income tax rate?
A: Nigeria's top PITA rate is 24%, applying to annual taxable income (after CRA
and other deductions) above NGN 3,200,000. The effective rate on gross salary
is typically in the 8–18% range for most formal sector employees.

Q: When is Nigeria's tax year?
A: January 1 to December 31. Monthly PAYE remittances are due by the 10th of
the following month. Annual PAYE returns (Form H1) are due by 31 January.
Individual income tax returns are due by 31 March.

Q: What is the NHF contribution and how does it work?
A: Employees in the formal sector contribute 2.5% of basic monthly salary to
the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria (FMBN). Mandatory for employers with 10 or
more employees. Contributions are tax-deductible and accumulate in a member
account. Contributors can access NHF mortgage loans at 6% fixed interest for
residential property purchase in Nigeria.

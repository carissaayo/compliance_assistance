National Housing Fund (NHF) — Guide for Employers and Employees
================================================================
Source: Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria (FMBN)
Legal Basis: NHF Act No. 3 of 1992

================================================================================
SECTION 1: AIMS AND OBJECTIVES
================================================================================

The National Housing Fund (NHF) was established to:

- Mobilise funds for the provision of affordable residential houses for Nigerians.
- Require Commercial and Merchant Banks to invest 10% of their loans and
  advances portfolio in housing.
- Require Insurance Companies to invest 20% of non-life and 40% of life funds
  in the housing sector, with 50% of these directly in the NHF.
- Channel Federal Government financial contributions into housing.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: FUNDING SOURCES
================================================================================

The NHF is funded by:

1. Mandatory contribution of 2.5% of monthly income of Nigerians earning
   NGN 3,000 and above per annum (from employees).
2. 10% of loans and advances portfolio from Commercial and Merchant Banks.
3. Federal Government financial contributions.

================================================================================
SECTION 3: CONTRIBUTION RATE
================================================================================

- Rate: 2.5% of monthly basic salary/income
- Who must contribute: All Nigerians in employment (whether self-employed or
  in paid employment) earning NGN 3,000 or above per annum.
- Mandatory for: Employers with 10 or more employees (private sector) and all
  public sector employees.
- Deduction: Monthly, at source by the employer.
- Tax treatment: NHF contributions are TAX-DEDUCTIBLE (deducted before PAYE
  brackets are applied under PITA).

================================================================================
SECTION 4: BENEFITS OF THE NHF
================================================================================

- Housing loan of up to 90% of the cost of the house.
- Interest on loans remains fixed throughout the life of the mortgage at 6% p.a.
- Long repayment period of up to 30 years.
- Contributions can serve as additional old age security.
- Up to NGN 50 million can be borrowed.
- Refunds with 2% interest on retirement.
- Monthly loan repayment is approximately the same as a typical monthly rent.
- Every contributor receives:
  - A lifetime registration number
  - A passbook for personal recording of contributions
  - Annual account statement

================================================================================
SECTION 5: ELIGIBILITY
================================================================================

To be eligible for NHF benefits:
- Register via NHF Form 1 (employer) and NHF Form 2 (employee).
- Deduct and remit monthly contributions to FMBN promptly (at least 6 months
  of contributions must have been made).
- Maintain a passbook updated by the employer (transferable from one employment
  to another).
- Receive a yearly statement of cumulative contributions plus accrued interest.
- Apply through any accredited Primary Mortgage Bank (PMB).
- Applicant must provide satisfactory evidence of regular income.

================================================================================
SECTION 6: PROCEDURE FOR REGISTRATION
================================================================================

All Nigerians in employment, whether self-employed or in paid employment, are
required by the NHF Act No. 3 of 1992 to contribute 2.5% of their monthly
salary/income to the fund.

Registration steps:

1. FMBN supplies Employer Registration Form (NHF 1) to employers.
2. Employer completes Form NHF 1 and returns to the nearest FMBN branch.
3. FMBN registers the employer and issues an employer registration number.
4. FMBN issues Form NHF 2 to employers for completion by employees (or
   directly to self-employed individuals).
5. Employee completes Form NHF 2 and returns it to the employer.
6. Employer (or self-employed individual) returns completed Form NHF 2 to FMBN.
7. FMBN registers employees on Form NHF 2 and allocates participation numbers.
8. FMBN issues a passbook to each registered employee where monthly deductions
   are posted by the employer.
9. Employer commences deduction of contributions at source and remits with a
   schedule of payment indicating amounts and period covered.
10. Self-employed contributors commence monthly remittances of contributions
    to FMBN.
11. FMBN accepts contributions and issues receipts for payments.

================================================================================
SECTION 7: CONDITIONS FOR REFUND
================================================================================

A contributor is entitled to a refund of NHF contributions in the following
circumstances:

- Has attained 60 years of age, OR has put in 35 years in service.
- Obtained a loan, fully liquidated it, AND has attained 60 years or 35 years
  in service.
- Has retired and is incapable of continuing contributions (medical grounds).

Retirement age by profession:
  - Professors, Judges, and Medical Consultants: 70 years
  - Academic, Non-Academic Staff, Judicial Staff: 65 years
  - Military Officers: 62 years

- Refund is made to the next-of-kin in case of a deceased contributor.

================================================================================
SECTION 8: DOCUMENTS REQUIRED FOR REFUND OF CONTRIBUTION
================================================================================

To apply for a refund of NHF contributions, the following documents are required:

1. Application letter requesting refund
2. Letter of offer of appointment
3. Birth certificate or Declaration of age certificate
4. Completed NHF Contribution withdrawal forms
5. Bank account details for payment
6. Medical report (if incapable of continuing contributions)
7. Letter of administration and Death Certificate (if applicable)
8. Evidence of contribution (receipt for self-employed contributor)
9. Biometric Verification Number (BVN)

================================================================================
SECTION 9: RELATIONSHIP WITH PAYE AND PENSION
================================================================================

NHF vs. Pension (CPS):
  - NHF is a HOUSING-SPECIFIC fund — not a retirement fund.
  - Pension (CPS) is a RETIREMENT fund managed by PFAs licensed by PenCom.
  - Both are separate mandatory deductions and both are tax-deductible.

NHF and PAYE:
  - NHF contribution (2.5% of basic salary) is deducted BEFORE PAYE brackets
    are applied.
  - This reduces the employee's taxable income, resulting in lower PAYE.

Summary of mandatory payroll deductions for a private sector employee:
  | Deduction          | Rate              | Base              | Tax Treatment   |
  |--------------------|-------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
  | Pension (CPS)      | 8% (employee)     | Total emoluments  | Tax-deductible  |
  | NHF                | 2.5%              | Basic salary      | Tax-deductible  |
  | NSITF              | 1% (employer only)| Total emoluments  | Employer cost   |
  | PAYE               | 7% – 24%          | Taxable income    | N/A             |

Note: NSITF is an employer-only contribution and does not reduce employee pay.

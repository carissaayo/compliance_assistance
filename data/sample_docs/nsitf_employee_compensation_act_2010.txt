NSITF — Employee's Compensation Act 2010 (No. 13 of 2010)
==========================================================
Source: Federal Republic of Nigeria Official Gazette
Legal Basis: Employees' Compensation Act, 2010, No. 13
Administering Body: Nigeria Social Insurance Trust Fund (NSITF) Management Board
Note: This file contains Parts 1, 2 (employer obligations only), and 6 of the Act,
which are directly relevant to employer payroll compliance and contribution obligations.

================================================================================
PART I — PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS
================================================================================

SECTION 1 — OBJECTIVES OF THE ACT
-----------------------------------
The objectives of the Act are to:

(a) Provide for an open and fair system of guaranteed and adequate compensation
    for all employees or their dependants for any death, injury, disease or
    disability arising out of or in the course of employment.

(b) Provide rehabilitation to employees with work-related disabilities as
    provided in this Act.

(c) Establish and maintain a solvent compensation fund managed in the interest
    of employees and employers.

(d) Provide for fair and adequate assessments for employers.

(e) Provide an appeal procedure that is simple, fair and accessible, with
    minimal delays.

(f) Combine efforts and resources of relevant stakeholders for the prevention
    of workplace disabilities, including the enforcement of occupational safety
    and health standards.

SECTION 2 — SCOPE AND APPLICATION
------------------------------------
(1) Subject to the provisions of sections 3 and 70 of this Act, this Act shall
apply to ALL EMPLOYERS AND EMPLOYEES in the public and private sectors in
the Federal Republic of Nigeria.

(2) The Nigeria Social Insurance Trust Fund Management Board (referred to as
"the Board") shall have power to implement this Act and the Fund established
under section 56 of this Act.

SECTION 3 — EXEMPTIONS
------------------------
Without prejudice to the generality of section 2, this Act shall NOT apply to
any member of the armed forces of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, other than
a person employed in a civilian capacity.

================================================================================
PART II — EMPLOYER REPORTING OBLIGATIONS (Selected)
================================================================================

SECTION 5 — EMPLOYER'S OBLIGATION TO REPORT DEATH, INJURY OR DISEASE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) An employer shall report to the Board and the nearest office of the National
Council for Occupational Safety and Health in the State WITHIN 7 DAYS of its
occurrence, every injury to an employee that arises out of and in the course of
employment.

(2) An employer shall report to the Board, within 7 days of receiving information
under section 4 of this Act, every disabling occupational disease or claim for
or allegation of an occupational disease.

(3) An employer shall IMMEDIATELY report the death of an employee arising out
of and in the course of employment to the Board and to the local representative
of the Board.

(4) A report under this section shall state:
    (a) the name and address of the employee;
    (b) the time and place of the disease, injury or death;
    (c) the nature of the injury or alleged injury;
    (d) the name and address of any specialist or accredited medical practitioner
        who attended to the employee; and
    (e) any other particular required by the Board.

(5) Failure to make a required report, unless allowed by the Board for sufficient
reason, constitutes an offence under this Act.

SECTION 14 — CONTRIBUTION BY EMPLOYEES FOR COMPENSATION PROHIBITED
-------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) No employer shall, either directly or indirectly, deduct from the remuneration
of an employee any part of a sum which the employer is or may become liable
to pay into the Fund, or require or permit the employee to contribute in any
manner towards indemnifying the employer against liability under this Act.

(2) Any person who contravenes sub-section (1) commits an offence and shall:
    (a) be liable on conviction to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 1 year,
        or a fine of not less than NGN 100,000, or both; AND
    (b) repay to the employee any sum which has been so deducted from the
        employee's remuneration.

(3) Where the offence was committed by a body corporate, it shall:
    (a) be liable on conviction to a fine of not less than NGN 1,000,000; AND
    (b) repay to the employee any sum so deducted.

IMPORTANT: NSITF contributions are an EMPLOYER-ONLY obligation.
           Employers may NOT deduct NSITF contributions from employee salaries.

PART VI — EMPLOYERS' ASSESSMENT AND CONTRIBUTIONS
================================================================================

SECTION 33 — EMPLOYERS' CONTRIBUTION TO THE FUND
--------------------------------------------------
(1) Every employer shall, within the first 2 years of the commencement of this
Act, make a MINIMUM MONTHLY CONTRIBUTION OF 1.0% (one percent) of the total
monthly payroll into the Fund.

(2) The Board shall, from time to time, make regulations prescribing:
    (a) the categorization of risk factors of each class or sub-class of industry,
        sector or workplace and the amount of contributions to be made into the
        Fund; and
    (b) different assessment rates applicable to each class and sub-class of
        industry, sector or workplace.

CONTRIBUTION RATE SUMMARY:
  Rate:  Minimum 1% of total monthly payroll
  Paid by: EMPLOYER ONLY (employees do not contribute)
  Base:  Total monthly payroll / total emoluments
  Purpose: Workplace injury compensation insurance

SECTION 34 — ASSESSMENTS
--------------------------
(1) The Board shall assess employers for such sums in such manner, form and
procedure as the Board may determine.

(2) Assessments shall, in the first instance, be based upon estimates of:
    (a) the employer's payroll for the year (provided under section 39); or
    (b) as determined by the Board under its merit rating system.

(3) The Board may, by order, establish a minimum assessment.

SECTION 35 — ASSESSMENT PAYABLE
---------------------------------
(1) Payment of any assessment made under section 33 shall be due on
1 January in the year for which it relates.

(2) The Board may provide for payment of assessment by installment, payable
on dates determined by the Board.

SECTION 36 — LIABILITY FOR ASSESSMENT
---------------------------------------
(1) The Board shall have a cause of action for any unpaid assessment and shall
be entitled to costs of any action to recover unpaid assessment.

(2) If, for any reason, an employer liable to assessment is not assessed by the
Board, the employer shall still be liable for the amount for which they should
have been assessed.

SECTION 37 — ASSESSMENT RATES
--------------------------------
(1) The Board may provide for different assessment rates applicable to each
class and sub-class of industry.

(2) Publication in the Federal Gazette of percentages and rates fixed by the
Board applicable to specific industries constitutes an assessment upon, and
notice to, each employer in those industries.

SECTION 38 — EMPLOYER CLASSIFICATION
--------------------------------------
(1) The Board may establish classes and sub-classes of industries.

(2) The Board shall assign every employer to an appropriate class or sub-class
based upon the industry in which the employer operates.

(3) The Board may by regulations create, divide, consolidate, rearrange, or
transfer employers and industries between classes and sub-classes.

SECTION 39 — EMPLOYER TO PROVIDE ESTIMATE OF PAYROLLS
-------------------------------------------------------
(1) Every employer shall:
    (a) keep complete and accurate particulars of the employer's payrolls at
        all times at a place in Nigeria, the address of which has been given
        to the Board;
    (b) furnish to the Board:
        (i)  when the employer becomes an employer within the scope of this Act;
        (ii) at other times as required by regulations — an estimate of the
             probable amount of the payroll of each of the employer's industries;
        (iii) provide signed copies of reports of the employer's payrolls, not
              later than 31 December in each year or at such other times as
              required by the Board.

(2) Where the employer fails to comply, the employer is liable to pay a penalty
percentage of the assessment as prescribed by regulations, and the Board may
make its own estimate of the payrolls and assess on that estimate.

(4) If an employer does not comply, or if a statement is not true and accurate,
the employer shall be liable to:
    - Imprisonment for a term not exceeding 1 year, OR
    - A fine of not less than NGN 100,000 (individual), OR
    - A fine of not less than NGN 1,000,000 (body corporate), AND
    - Each director, manager or officer of the body corporate shall be deemed
      to have committed the offence.

SECTION 40 — STATEMENT AND ESTIMATE OF EARNINGS
-------------------------------------------------
(1) Every employer shall, not later than the LAST DAY OF FEBRUARY each year,
provide to the Board a statement of:
    (a) the total amount of all earnings paid to its employees in the preceding year;
    (b) an estimate of the earnings that will be paid to its employees in the
        current year;
    (c) the nature of the work activities; and
    (d) any additional information as the Board may require.

(2) If the statement is found to be incorrect, the Board shall reassess the
employer for each year the statement was incorrectly made and may charge
interest on any assessment not paid.

(3) Employers shall declare the earnings of employees employed outside Nigeria
as if they were employed in Nigeria.

SECTION 41 — EMPLOYER'S EXPERIENCE ACCOUNTS
---------------------------------------------
(1) The Board shall maintain experience accounts for each employer, indicating
the assessments levied and the cost of all claims chargeable in respect of
the employer.

SECTION 42 — SUPER ASSESSMENT
-------------------------------
(1) If, in any year, the claims cost charged to an employer's experience account
exceeds 105% of the ordinary assessment, the Board may assess a super-assessment
of up to 133% of the employer's ordinary assessment for that year.

SECTION 43 — MERIT RATING
---------------------------
The Board may adopt a system of merit rating for employers.

SECTION 44 — LIABILITY FOR INDEPENDENT CONTRACTORS AND SUB-CONTRACTORS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Where any person or organization employs an independent contractor to
perform work in a workplace, BOTH the organization and the independent
contractor shall be jointly liable for any assessment under this Act.

(2) Where work is performed under a sub-contract, the principal, contractor
and sub-contractor shall be jointly liable for any assessment.

SECTION 45 — SECURITY FOR PAYMENT OF ASSESSMENT
-------------------------------------------------
(1) The Board may serve notice on an employer requiring the employer to provide
security for the payment of assessments.

(3) The employer shall, within 30 days after being served with notice, provide
the required security.

SECTION 46 — PENALTY FOR NON-PAYMENT OF ASSESSMENT
----------------------------------------------------
If an assessment is not paid when required, or security is not provided when
required, the Board may assess a PENALTY of an amount equal to 10% of the
unpaid assessment or the value of the security required.

SECTION 47 — OFFENCES
-----------------------
If an employer:
    (a) defaults in providing the security required by the Board;
    (b) defaults in the payment of any amount due to the Fund; or
    (c) contravenes any decisions of the Board,
the employer commits an offence under this Act.

SECTION 48 — PROVISIONAL ASSESSMENT
-------------------------------------
(1) Where an employer fails to comply with section 39, or the information
provided does not reflect the probable payroll amount, the Board may assess
such sum as it considers to be the probable payroll of the employer.

(2) If the provisional assessment differs from the required assessment, the
employer or the Board shall be liable to pay the other the difference.

SECTION 49 — TEMPORARY INDUSTRY
---------------------------------
Where an employer engages in an industry within the scope of this Act and has
not been assessed, the Board may require the employer to pay or give security
for the payment to the Fund of a sum sufficient to pay the assessment for
which the employer may be liable.

SECTION 50 — EMPLOYER COMMENCING OR RECOMMENCING A BUSINESS
-------------------------------------------------------------
When an employer commences or recommences a business, the employer shall
provide, WITHIN 30 DAYS of commencement or recommencement, to the Board
the statements required under section 51 of this Act.

SECTION 51 — EMPLOYER CEASING TO BE AN EMPLOYER
-------------------------------------------------
A person who ceases to be an employer shall notify the Board WITHIN 30 DAYS
of cessation and shall provide a statement of the total payroll for the year.

SECTION 52 — RECORDS OF REMUNERATION AND OPERATIONS
-----------------------------------------------------
Every employer shall keep an accurate account of all remuneration paid to its
employees and of any other particular of its operations as may be required by
the Board.

SECTION 53 — EXAMINATION (BOARD AUDIT POWERS)
-----------------------------------------------
(1) Any person authorized by the Board may examine the books and accounts
of any employer as the Board deems necessary for administering this Act.

(2) For the purposes of administration of this Act, any authorized person may,
at all reasonable hours, enter any part of the establishment of any employer.

(3) An employer shall produce, within 30 days of receiving notice from the Board,
all documents, deeds, papers and computer records relating to the subject
matter of an examination.

(6) An employer who obstructs an examination or refuses to produce documents
commits an offence.

SECTION 54 — ENFORCEMENT POWERS
---------------------------------
An officer of the Board may, for the purposes of carrying out duties under this Act:
    (a) enter any workplace at any time with or without warrant or notice;
    (b) require production of any licence, document, record or report;
    (c) remove documents for the purpose of making copies or extracts;
    (d) make enquiries of any person in a workplace; and
    (e) require that records not be disturbed for a reasonable period during
        an examination, investigation or enquiry.

SECTION 55 — RIGHT OF APPEAL
------------------------------
A person aggrieved by any decision of the Board may appeal to the Board for
a review of such decision.
